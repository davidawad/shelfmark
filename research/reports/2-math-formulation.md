## Which formulation fits this problem best, and what is still open

Best fit (inference from the table): a two-term objective on an order-preserving tree.

1. Similarity term: Dasgupta cost on a similarity matrix over the 8,500 works (embedding or citation or both). It already has the property wanted here (penalizes both flat and incoherent trees), has provable approximations (average linkage / spreading-metric LP), and has a differentiable version (HypHC). Compute: trivial at n=8,500.
2. Navigation term: Knuth/Hu-Tucker-style expected depth under a usage prior p_i (what is actually pulled off the shelf), plus an explicit cost per branching decision (fan-out) so the optimum fan-out is finite. This is what removes the "everything under root" degeneracy. Order-preserving trees make the leaf order the physical shelf order.
3. Naming and sanity: LLM labeling (TaxoAdapt / Chain-of-Layer style) on the finished tree; score candidates with the Unterkalmsteiner attributes and the reference-free robustness measure.
4. If he wants the depth to fall out of evidence instead of a hand penalty: MDL / hierarchical SBM / nCRP instead of term 2.

Still open (nothing found that addresses it):
- A published single objective that combines pairwise-similarity coherence (Dasgupta) with an explicit query-weighted, fan-out-penalized navigation cost. The pieces exist separately; I found no paper unifying them. (Absence in what I searched, not proof of absence.)
- Choosing facet order when a faceted scheme is linearized to one shelf. Priss formalizes facets as lattices, but I found no optimization of facet order.
- Hierarchies stable under insertion: TaxoExpan inserts into a tree, but no objective trades off optimality against not re-shelving books.
- Empirical validation of human navigation cost on such trees (user studies on menu depth exist but UNVERIFIED here).

Query-distribution-independent optimal hierarchy: I found no theorem that states exactly "no universal optimal hierarchy exists", but three results point to it. (a) Kleinberg's impossibility theorem: no clustering function (hence no tree extraction rule) satisfies all of scale invariance, richness, consistency. (b) Ugly Duckling: with no prior over features, no classification is privileged. (c) Dasgupta/Cohen-Addad: the optimal tree is defined relative to a similarity function w; change w (i.e. the notion of relevant similarity, the "query distribution") and the optimum changes. Any claimed universal optimum therefore has to assume a fixed w or p, which is exactly the query-distribution dependence. Strictly, these are a relativity argument (inference), not one formal theorem.
