# Scoring catalogue, 2026-10-10

Every score of `research/reports/6-scoring-catalogue.md`, computed by
`shelfmark-bench scores` for 28 runs:

- 13 schemes plus the learned scheme, shelfmark-placed on the arXiv corpus
  (14 runs) and the general schemes plus the learned scheme on the book
  holdout (7 runs).
- 5 gold runs (arXiv, MSC and JEL on the papers; LCC and LCC-full on the
  books). ACM CCS has no gold: the corpus carries CCS 1998 codes only (see
  `../2026-10-10-r3/`).
- 2 learned "own" runs: in-sample placements and holdout insertion.

`REPRODUCE.md` gives the commands. `results.json` holds every score, its
95% bootstrap interval (200 replicates), the null-model and noise summaries,
inter-classifier consistency, all input hashes and the agreement matrices.

**Inputs are the published ones.** The committed graphs are reused. The
placements are not committed, so they were regenerated with the r2 commands.
All 19 placed and 5 gold harness results reproduce the published
`../2026-10-10-r2/`, `../2026-10-10-r3/` and `../2026-10-10/` files exactly
(same tree SHA-256 and cost_norm). So do the learned runs
`learned-own` and `learned-own-holdout` (`../2026-10-10-learned/runs/`). The
catalogue recomputes Dasgupta cost independently and checks that it equals
the harness value for every run. The embedding vectors (for cophenetic
correlation, silhouette, Davies–Bouldin and the centroid probe) were
regenerated with the pinned model and are pinned by hash in `results.json`.

**Caveats.**
- `learned-placed` and `learned-own` are in-sample: the learned scheme was
  fitted to this corpus graph. That explains its lead on every graph score;
  its out-of-sample result is in `../2026-10-10-learned/`.
- JEL gold has 2 labelled papers and is shown only for completeness. It is
  left out of the agreement analysis.
- The gold group mixes the two corpora and has only 6 runs, so its rank
  correlations are close to ±1 by construction. Read them as indicative.
- Against the external gold, arXiv on the papers and LCC on the books are
  self-comparisons.
- MDL is dominated by the scheme term for large schemes (LCC-full 7.8 Mbit,
  IPC 1.3 Mbit, against 10⁴ items).

## Conclusions

**Which scores agree.** The groups below come from the 14 placed runs on the
papers; the holdout and gold groups show the same blocks, more weakly.

1. *Graph-fit block.* Dasgupta cost_norm, its depth-1 cut, tree-sampling
   divergence, triplet accuracy, AMI and hierarchical F against arXiv
   categories, and V-measure: |ρ| = 0.7–0.9 with each other. The binarized
   cost joins this block more loosely (ρ = 0.7 with cost_norm).
   This block is largely driven by coverage (ρ(cost_norm, coverage) = −0.8):
   unplaced items meet at the root.
2. *Shelf-order block.* Shelf locality and its Kendall τ (ρ = 0.9), author
   collocation (ρ = 0.7) and the kNN and centroid probes (0.6–0.8).
3. *Size block.* MDL bits per item, expected search bits, literary warrant
   and insertion splits (|ρ| = 0.7–0.9). These track how many classes a
   scheme has and uses.
4. *Concentration block.* Dendrogram purity (fine and coarse), largest-class
   share (ρ = +0.8 with DP fine), cophenetic correlation and silhouette.
   Dendrogram purity rewards a few large classes as much as good nesting.

**Which disagree.**
- Dasgupta cost and shelf locality are close to independent across schemes
  (ρ = +0.2, where a positive sign means disagreement). A scheme can group
  neighbours under a low LCA and still scatter them along the shelf: UNESCO
  and IPC have the third- and fourth-best cost but locality 0.12 and 0.06.
  The reverse also happens: MSC-placed has the second-best locality (0.27)
  and the worst cost.
- Dasgupta cost and dendrogram purity agree only moderately (ρ = −0.5).
  Dendrogram purity sides with the concentration block.
- Hospitality anti-correlates with probe accuracy and locality (ρ ≈ −0.7).
  Hospitable decimal notations (LCC, LCC-full, ILC2, IPC) are the large
  schemes, which place papers finely and less predictably.
- Notation length and Gini correlate with almost nothing (|ρ| ≤ 0.5). They
  describe the scheme rather than how well it fits the corpus.

**Which rankings are robust.**
- Stable on the papers. Dasgupta cost_norm (bootstrap τ 0.99, 5th percentile
  0.98; τ 0.93 at 20% placement noise), TSD (0.99, noise 1.0), triplet
  accuracy (0.98, noise 0.96), shelf locality (0.97, noise 0.98), kNN probe
  (0.98, noise 0.93) and V-measure (0.98).
- Unstable. Dendrogram purity (τ 0.75 fine, 0.69 coarse, 5th percentiles
  0.58 and 0.49) and, less so, hierarchical F (0.85).
- On the 500-book holdout only cost_norm (0.97), triplet (0.95) and TSD
  (0.92) rank stably. Dendrogram purity (0.19) and locality (0.62) do not.
- Null models. On the papers every placed scheme beats shuffled labels on
  cost by |z| > 49 (arXiv-placed −50.0, the weakest). Against a random tree of the same shape, ILC2 (z = −14.1),
  OpenAlex topics, MSC, the learned scheme (−8.3 to −8.5) and UNESCO (−7.5)
  gain clearly from their nesting. arXiv-placed (+0.5) and BK (−0.3) gain
  nothing beyond their shape.

**Inter-classifier consistency is low.** Against the source labels,
shelfmark's exact class matches 6.8% of arXiv papers (κ 0.06; 49% agree on the
top-level group and 44% on the archive), 4.6% of MSC papers, and 0.4% (LCC-full) to 2.7% (LCC) of the
books. The learned scheme's shelfmark placements match its own placements
on 30% of papers (89% at depth 1).
